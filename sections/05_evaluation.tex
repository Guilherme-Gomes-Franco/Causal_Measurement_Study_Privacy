\subsection{Dataset Integrity \& CMP Consent Yield}
\label{subsec:observability_yield}

Applying the Paired Observability Filter (Section~\ref{subsec:curation}) extracted between 33 and 54 actively observable publisher auctions per configuration ($22.0\%$ to $36.0\%$ Prebid transparency). Crucially, the 3-Tier Consent Clicker successfully negotiated European privacy gates across the crawl:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{chrome\_baseline}: 107 domains resolved ($71.3\%$ auto-accepted; 61 regex, 43 CSS, 3 API);
    \item \texttt{firefox\_hardened}: 118 domains resolved ($78.7\%$ auto-accepted; 64 regex, 53 CSS, 1 API);
    \item \texttt{brave\_hardened}: 114 domains resolved ($76.0\%$ auto-accepted; 59 regex, 51 CSS, 4 API).
\end{itemize}
The unhandled minority ($21.3\%$--$32.0\%$) comprised non-EU sites lacking consent banners or publishers serving custom, non-standard modal overlays.

\paragraph{Negative Control Validation (\texttt{chrome\_baseline})}
In unhardened Chrome, clearing stateful storage resulted in a negligible median bid shift of \textbf{$-1.12\%$} ($p = 0.4832$, fail to reject $H_0$). The distribution displayed massive variance ($\text{IQR} = 198.11\%$), with the third quartile surging to $+123.11\%$. Over $60.61\%$ of domains re-identified the client within the noise threshold ($\rid = 0.606$). 

While individual domains saw drops (e.g., \url{allrecipes.com} dropping from $\$10.00 \to \$0.00$), other domains maintained or increased bids (e.g., \url{archdaily.com} clearing at $\$26.00$), demonstrating that commercial DSPs routinely bridge stateful resets using stateless device graphs.

\paragraph{Baseline Defenses (\texttt{firefox\_baseline} \& \texttt{brave\_baseline})}
Standard Firefox (ETP Standard) produced a minor median shift of \textbf{$-4.11\%$} ($p = 0.3445$, $\rid = 0.543$). Standard blocklists do not restrict active Canvas or Audio API calls, leaving identity graphs intact.

Standard Brave (default Farbling) reduced median bids by \textbf{$-21.39\%$}, reducing the median clearing price from $\$0.15 \to \$0.08$. However, the upper quartile remained positive ($Q_3 = +46.95\%$), showing that farbling alone introduces algorithmic valuation uncertainty (bid shading) without completely severing device linkage.

\paragraph{The Stealth Optimum (\texttt{firefox\_hardened})}
Hardened Firefox achieved a \textbf{$-26.86\%$} median bid reduction, collapsing the median clearing price from $\$0.09$ to \textbf{\$0.02} (a $77.8\%$ absolute median decline). Crucially, the upper quartile compressed to \textbf{$Q_3 = +1.20\%$}: over $75\%$ of evaluated domains experienced price drops or near-zero shifts. 

The paired Wilcoxon test confirmed statistical significance at the marginal $\alpha = 0.10$ level ($W = 472.0, p = \textbf{0.0734}$). This was achieved with zero measurable usability breakage ($\ucost = 0.00$).

\paragraph{The Apex Disruption (\texttt{brave\_hardened})}
Hardened Brave produced the most severe economic drop: a median price collapse of \textbf{$-59.98\%$}, dropping the median clearing bid from $\$0.15 \to \$0.06$. The distribution demonstrates substantial tracking severance:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Total Collapse in $Q_1$:} The first quartile dropped to \textbf{$-100.00\%$}, indicating that over $25\%$ of active domains experienced complete auction wipeouts (e.g., \url{dailymail.co.uk} falling from $\$0.07 \to \$0.00$, \url{cricbuzz.com} from $\$0.01 \to \$0.00$).
    \item \textbf{Negative Upper Quartile Ceiling:} The third quartile was capped at \textbf{$Q_3 = 0.00\%$}. Exactly $75\%$ of all evaluated domains saw non-positive bid deltas ($\le 0.00\%$), driving the re-identification rate down to $\rid = 0.500$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Ad-Tech Pricing \& Non-Parametric Rank Dilution}
\label{subsec:rank_dilution}

Despite Brave Hardened's $-59.98\%$ median collapse and $Q_3 \le 0.00\%$ compression, its paired Wilcoxon test yielded $W = 361.5, p = 0.1192$. This dynamic illustrates two critical properties of programmatic ad markets:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Left-Censored Reserve Floors:} SSP price floors enforce minimum clearing bids ($\$0.01$--$\$0.05$). Consequently, negative shifts cannot decay below zero, truncating the absolute magnitude of negative differences ($|D_i| \le \$0.15$).
    \item \textbf{Heteroskedastic Video Ad Outliers (Rank Dilution):} The Wilcoxon Signed-Rank test ranks observations by the absolute magnitude of their dollar shift ($|D_i|$). On \url{standard.co.uk}, an un-targeted outstream video ad unit cleared at $\$0.04$ Pre-Break and spiked to $\$16.00$ Post-Break ($+41,137\%$). Because $|D_i| = \$15.96$, this single video outlier received the maximum possible rank ($R_i = 46$). In a sample of $N=46$, a single large positive outlier offsets dozens of consistent display ad drops where bids collapsed from $\$0.50 \to \$0.01$ ($|D_i| = \$0.49$).
\end{enumerate}
A similar dynamic occurred on \texttt{firefox\_hardened}, where a single video spike on \url{verywellhealth.com} ($\$0.10 \to \$22.00$) diluted the rank sum. Following the American Statistical Association (ASA) guidelines on $p$-values~\cite{wasserstein2016asa}, real-world economic significance must be evaluated via effect size and quartile compression ($Q_3 \le 0.00\%$) rather than rank-sum symmetry alone.

\subsection{Silo 2: Identity Diffusion \& The Retry Storm}
\label{subsec:silo_network}

Decomposing Identity Diffusion ($D_{\text{ID}}$) into Cookie Synchronizations ($S_{\text{post}}$) and Smuggled Navigational Identifiers ($U_{\text{post}}$) reveals an important operational insight:

Across \texttt{chrome\_baseline}, \texttt{firefox\_baseline}, and both Brave configurations, syncs and smuggled UIDs remained remarkably stable: $S_{\text{post}} \in [2,461, 3,302]$ and $U_{\text{post}} \in [18,359, 18,885]$.

In stark contrast, \texttt{firefox\_hardened} exhibited a massive surge: \textbf{Cookie Synchronizations more than doubled to $S_{\text{post}} = 6,006$}, and \textbf{Smuggled UIDs surged to $U_{\text{post}} = 22,970$}, driving total diffusion events to $D_{\text{ID}} = 28,976$. 

Inspection of proxy logs reveals this was driven by a \textbf{stateless blocking retry storm}: when Firefox's multi-layered defenses (Total Cookie Protection, RFP canvas blanking, and query parameter stripping) blocked tracker endpoints and sanitized link decorations, client-side tracking scripts threw exceptions and repeatedly re-triggered synchronization requests within recursive event loops. 

Crucially, deduplicating target endpoints revealed that Hardened Firefox communicated with the fewest distinct tracking hosts (\textbf{810 hosts}, vs. 872 in Chrome and 888 in Brave Hardened). Thus, while request *frequency* spiked due to client-side retries, Firefox successfully restricted the *diffusion* of identifiers across unique commercial entities.

\subsection{Silo 3: Usability Cost \& Runtime Breakage}
\label{subsec:silo_usability}

Client-side error logging captured 45,000+ unhandled runtime JavaScript exceptions across our crawls. Cross-engine normalization reveals key differences between defense approaches:

\paragraph{Gecko Engine Performance}
Standard Firefox recorded 1,590 exceptions, while Hardened Firefox recorded 2,400 exceptions ($+50.9\%$). However, because RFP's timer quantization and canvas blanking return syntactically valid mock objects, these errors did not alter DOM layout trees or break page scripts, preserving a usability penalty of $\ucost = 0.00$.

\paragraph{Chromium Engine Performance}
Chrome Baseline recorded 12,816 exceptions. Brave Baseline logged 13,309 exceptions ($\ucost = 0.31$), and Brave Hardened logged 14,882 exceptions ($\ucost = 0.44$). 

Brave's aggressive network-layer request blocking severs script dependency chains, causing downstream publisher scripts to throw unhandled \texttt{TypeError} and null-pointer exceptions that degrade site features.
