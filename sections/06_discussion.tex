\section{Discussion: The Usability Trap \& Metric Refinement}
\label{sec:discussion}

The empirical findings in Section~\ref{sec:evaluation} expose an operational paradox: defensive blocking triggers network retry storms that artificially inflate raw request volumes, while aggressive request filtering imposes measurable usability breakage. 
Here, we deconstruct why naive composite metrics break under these dynamics, present the axiomatic derivation of the Refined Effective Tracking Resistance metric ($\etr$), and map the empirical Pareto frontier governing browser privacy.

\subsection{Deconstructing the Usability Trap}
\label{subsec:usability_trap}

Initial privacy measurement frameworks often model the trade-off between tracking mitigation and usability via intuitive composite quotients:
\begin{equation}
\etrinit = \frac{(1 - \rid) \times \left( \frac{1}{\ln(D_{\text{ID}} + e)} \right)}{1 + \ucost}
\label{eq:etr_initial}
\end{equation}
Evaluating our empirical corpus under Equation~\ref{eq:etr_initial} produced an anomalous, inverted ranking:
\begin{align*}
\etrinit: \; & \text{FF Base (0.046)} > \text{FF Hard (0.042)} > \text{Chrome (0.040)} \\
& > \text{Brave Base (0.035)} > \text{Brave Hard (0.035)}
\end{align*}
This formulation ranked unhardened Chrome ($0.0395$) \textit{higher} than Brave Hardened ($0.0347$), despite the latter reducing median clearing prices by $60\%$ and capping the upper quartile at $Q_3 = 0.00\%$. 
Forensic analysis revealed three mathematical pathologies:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Binary Disruption Insensitivity:} The binary threshold ($\Delta\cpm \ge -10\%$) creates a step-function discontinuity where a minor drop of $-11\%$ is treated identically to complete bid suppression ($-100.0\%$), leaving Brave Hardened's median collapse ($-59.98\%$) and $25\%$ bid suppression rate uncaptured by the numerator;
    \item \textbf{Linear Denominator Penalty:} The linear denominator ($1 + \ucost = 1.44$) penalized Brave Hardened by $44\%$, disproportionately degrading its score despite substantial tracking disruption;
    \item \textbf{The Retry-Storm Inversion:} Equation~\ref{eq:etr_initial} uses raw diffusion events ($D_{\text{ID}}$) in the logarithmic denominator. Blocking tracker endpoints triggers client-side retry storms: tracking scripts repeatedly re-trigger cookie synchronization~\cite{papadopoulos2019cookie} and UID decoration loops~\cite{randall2022measuring} within recursive event handlers~\cite{iqbal2020adgraph}. 
    This inflated Hardened Firefox's volume to 28,976 total events (6,006 cookie syncs and 22,970 smuggled UIDs), penalizing the browser for effectively blocking third-party endpoints.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Axiomatic Formulation of $\etr$}
\label{subsec:etr_star}

To resolve these distortions, we formulate the \textbf{Refined Effective Tracking Resistance Metric} ($\etr$):
\begin{equation}
\etr = \frac{\Phi_{\text{econ}} \times \Psi_{\text{containment}}}{\Omega_{\text{usability}}}
\label{eq:etr_refined}
\end{equation}
where each component satisfies strict axiomatic requirements:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Continuous Economic Disruption ($\Phi_{\text{econ}}$):} Integrates binary resistance, high-tier disruption, and median price decay: $\Phi_{\text{econ}} = \frac{1}{3} [(1 - \rid) + \htr + \max(0, -\widetilde{\Delta\cpm})]$, scaling monotonically in $[0, 1]$ with tracking disruption;
    \item \textbf{Entity Density Containment ($\Psi_{\text{containment}}$):} Evaluates unique tracking entities contacted per publisher: $\Psi_{\text{containment}} = 1 / \ln(\frac{D_{\text{entity}}}{N_{\text{sites}}} + e)$, rewarding browsers that restrict communication across distinct corporate tracking entities~\cite{bashir2016tracing, cook2020inferring} while remaining invariant to local script retry loops ($D_{\text{entity}} \to 0 \implies \Psi \to 1.0$);
    \item \textbf{Sub-Linear Usability Dampening ($\Omega_{\text{usability}}$):} Dampens the usability penalty logarithmically: $\Omega_{\text{usability}} = 1 + \ln(1 + \ucost)$, ensuring script exceptions do not disproportionately penalize real privacy gains.
\end{enumerate}

As demonstrated in Table~\ref{tab:etr_breakdown}, $\etr$ restores a consistent ranking:

As mapped in Figure~\ref{fig:pareto}, defense strategies define an empirical non-dominated frontier:
(1)~\textbf{The Stealth / Zero-Overhead Optimum (\texttt{firefox\_hardened}):} Combining Tor-derived API normalization (RFP)~\cite{tor_design, mozilla_rfp_docs} with Total Cookie Protection and query stripping~\cite{randall2022measuring} achieves $\Phi_{\text{econ}} = 0.368$ (a $-26.86\%$ median bid collapse) with \textbf{zero excess exception overhead ($\ucost = 0.00$)}, as mock return values allow publisher scripts to continue executing cleanly;
(2)~\textbf{The Maximum Privacy Optimum (\texttt{brave\_hardened}):} Combining API farbling~\cite{brave_fingerprinting_protections_2025} with aggressive network request blocking maximizes tracking severance ($\Phi_{\text{econ}} = 0.526$, $-59.98\%$ median collapse, $\rid = 0.500$), but incurs $\ucost = 0.44$ due to severed script dependency chains~\cite{iqbal2020adgraph}; and
(3)~\textbf{Dominated Configurations:} \nolinkurl{chrome_baseline}, \nolinkurl{firefox_baseline}, and \nolinkurl{brave_baseline} are strictly Pareto-dominated, yielding weaker privacy protections without offering offsetting usability advantages.

\subsection{Methodological Post-Mortem}
\label{subsec:post_mortem}

During platform validation, we identified a critical experimental failure mode: naive filesystem profile cloning leaked persistent identifiers across phases. 
Chromium caches session descriptors and HSTS tokens within binary LevelDB transaction logs and service worker quotas. In early test runs, unpurged LevelDB files preserved cross-phase identity state, producing artificial $0.0\%$ bid shifts.

This failure confirms empirical findings by Acar et al.~\cite{acar2014web} and Lerner et al.~\cite{lerner2016internet}, who demonstrated that commercial tracking networks exploit persistent HTML5 Web Storage~\cite{whatwg_webstorage} and HSTS supercookies to respawn deleted identifiers. Standard browser ``Clear Browsing Data'' routines are insufficient for rigorous web measurement; reliable causal interventions require deterministic profile sanitization that wipes underlying LevelDB stores while preserving anti-fingerprinting defenses (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_sanitizer}). 
While our causal pipeline and refined metric isolate stateless tracking along this Pareto boundary, several ecological constraints govern the generalizability of these findings, as examined next in Section~\ref{sec:threats}.
